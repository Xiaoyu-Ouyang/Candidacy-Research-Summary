\section{Background}
Since the formulation of quantum mechanics, understanding the quantum many-body physics, namely the emergent collective behavior arising from many interacting quantum particles, has become one of the central problems in physics, chemistry and material science. 
This problem becomes interesting, because the principle behind the quantum many-body system is simply the Schrodinger equation (plus the particle symmetry or Pauli exclusion principle?) $H \ket{\Psi(t)}=i \partial_t \ket{\Psi(t)}$, while the many-body wavefunction $\ket{\Psi}$ is in general of exponential complexity to the particle number $N$, thus impossible to precisely represent the state in classical computers for relatively large $N$ around 10 to 20.

In different settings, people will have different interest of the system. 
One major problem to solve is the ground state problem $H\ket{\Psi}=E\ket{\Psi}$ of the quantum many-body system, in which we only need the ground state energy $E_g$, sometimes also the ground state $\ket{\Psi_g}$. 
People have developed multiple methods to deal with ground state problem, based on the type of system and the accuracy needed, we have methods like Hartree-Fock, couple cluster (?), DMRG, ... \cite{}
In lots of systems, the ground state could exhibit the area-law entanglement, so we can use tensor network to efficiently represent it \cite{}. 
However, in the non-equilibrium setup, we would need to deal with the time evolution of the state $\ket{\Psi(t)}$, this state typically exhibit an exponential blowup of complexity, due to the dynamical growth of the entanglement entropy \cite{}.
